 Electric cars reach record 45% of 2025 registrations in Singapore

 Singapore’s automotive sector reached a historic turning point in 2025, as battery electric vehicles (EVs) became the most popular choice for new passenger cars. According to the Land Transport Authority (LTA), EVs accounted for a record 45.1% of all new registrations, officially overtaking petrol-electric hybrids.

 While EVs dominated new sales, the total national car fleet reflects a more gradual transition. As of the end of 2025, the total car population reached 659,889 vehicles. A breakdown by fuel type reveals the current makeup of the Singaporean fleet:

 Pure internal combustion engine (ICE): Traditional petrol and diesel cars still comprise 74.2% of the total fleet.

 Petrol-electric hybrids: This segment now accounts for 18.4% of all cars on the road.

 Battery electric vehicles: The pure electric car population reached 49,110 units by the end of 2025, accounting for 7.4% of the total car fleet. 

 This represents a significant leap from 2015, when there was only one registered electric car in the country.

 Brand dominance and market leadership

 The surge in adoption has upended traditional market hierarchies. Chinese manufacturer BYD has emerged as the clear leader, recording 11,184 registrations in 2025—a massive increase from the 1,416 registrations it saw in 2023.

 Other major players in the 2025 market include:

 Toyota: Maintained second place with 7,466 registrations.

 BMW: Followed with 5,091 new registrations.

 Mercedes Benz: Recorded 4,871 registrations.

 Tesla: Solidified its presence with 3,476 new registrations.

 Sector transitions and policy drivers

 The shift toward cleaner energy is also evident in public and commercial transport. The diesel taxi fleet has almost vanished, dropping from more than 24,000 in 2015 to just 84 in 2025. Most modern taxis now operate on petrol-electric engines, accounting for 11,547 of the remaining fleet. 

 Public buses are following a similar green trajectory. Electric buses increased from just one unit in 2016 to 799 by the end of 2025. During the same period, diesel-electric (plug-in) buses appeared for the first time in 2024 and reached 44 units by 2025.

 Motorcycles remain the most petrol-dependent category, with 151,228 out of 151,616 units still using petrol in 2025. However, electric motorcycles are beginning to emerge, rising from just two units in 2015 to 388 in 2025.

 In the “Goods & Other Vehicles” sector, diesel remains the dominant fuel, accounting for 122,576 units, though it has declined gradually from 136,686 in 2015. The adoption of electric goods vehicles is accelerating, rising from 39 units in 2018 to 6,389 in 2025.

 Singapore Green Plan 2030

 Singapore’s 2030 ICE policy is framed within the Singapore Green Plan 2030 and a commitment to phase out ICE vehicles by 2040, using 2030 as the key cut‑off for new registrations. From 2030, all new car and taxi registrations must be “cleaner‑energy models” – such as battery electric, plug‑in hybrid, hybrid, or other low‑ and zero‑emission technologies – meaning no new petrol‑only or diesel‑only cars can be registered after that date, while diesel car and taxi registrations already ceased from 2025. 

 Existing ICE vehicles can continue to operate beyond 2030 under Singapore’s 10‑year Certificate of Entitlement (COE) system, but the policy design assumes that, as COEs cycle out and as road‑tax and fuel‑duty disincentives tighten, most ICE vehicles will leave the fleet by around 2040.

 Singapore currently provides substantial but tapering fiscal incentives for electric vehicles, while incentives for hybrids are being removed as policy shifts towards fully electric drivetrains.  Under the EV Early Adoption Incentive (EEAI), buyers of fully electric cars and taxis registered in 2026 receive a 45% rebate on the Additional Registration Fee (ARF), capped at SGD 7,500 (USD 5,943). The scheme ends on December 31, 2026. From January 1, 2026, to December 31, 2027, only EVs qualify for the vehicular emissions scheme (VES) rebates. With the revised EEAI and VES, EV buyers can save up to SGD 30,000 (USD 23,772) off the ARF for cars registered in 2026, and up to SGD 20,000 (USD 15,848) in 2027.
