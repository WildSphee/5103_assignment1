Flash-sale pricing

One sale event, three products, one price each. Pick the prices, launch the sale, then run the same decision again and again to see how much luck is involved.

The retailer’s demand forecast

Expected units sold for each product, given its own price (rows) and the total of the three prices in the event (columns, shown as k, the number of price steps above the cheapest event). A product sells more when the other products are priced higher. A dash marks a combination that cannot occur. Edit any number to try your own forecast.

Stock on hand

Inventory is known before prices are set. The forecast cannot promise more units than the stock.

Best prices for this forecast

A quick check by trying all 125 price combinations. If you solve the integer program yourself, your answer should match the top row.

Optional: past sales history, for building your own forecast

Illustrative history from 300 earlier events with random prices and random stock levels. Sales are cut off when an item sold out, so the data hide some demand. Download it to fit a model of your own, then type the predicted values into the forecast above.
